You clipped a curb on a tight Vancouver side street, and now there's a grey scar running along the lip of your wheel. It happens constantly here — narrow parking, high curbs, and a lot of low-profile tires that leave the rim as the first thing to make contact.

The good news: curb rash is one of the most repairable kinds of wheel damage, and fixing it costs a fraction of replacing the wheel. Here's what to expect on price.

## What curb rash repair costs

Most curb rash falls into two buckets, and the price follows the depth of the damage rather than the size of the wheel.

| Damage type | What it looks like | Typical cost per wheel |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Light scuffing | Surface scratches through the finish, no missing metal | $50–$150 |
| Deep rash or gouges | Material removed, visible dents or chunks out of the lip | $150–$300+ |
| Diamond cut refinishing | Any rash on an OEM diamond-cut face | Quoted individually |

Two things push a repair toward the upper end. The first is finish type — a diamond-cut wheel has to be re-turned on a CNC lathe and re-cleared, which is a longer process than sanding and recoating a painted wheel. The second is structural damage. If the impact bent the rim or cracked it, that's straightening or TIG welding work before any cosmetic refinishing starts.

## What actually changes the price

**Finish type.** A solid painted or powder-coated wheel is the most straightforward to repair. Diamond-cut and polished faces cost more because they require machining, not just refinishing.

**Depth of the gouge.** Surface scratches sand out. Once the curb has removed metal, that material has to be built back up and re-profiled before the finish goes on.

**Number of wheels.** Doing all four at once is more economical per wheel than bringing in one, and it guarantees the finish matches across the set — worth thinking about if two or three already have wear.

**Structural damage.** Bends, hairline cracks, and out-of-round rims add repair steps. We check structural integrity on every repair rather than just covering damage cosmetically.

## Repair or replace?

Replacement is almost always the more expensive path. A single replacement OEM alloy typically costs many times what a cosmetic repair does, before you add mounting and balancing — and prices vary enormously by vehicle, so it's worth pricing your specific wheel before assuming. For ordinary curb damage the math rarely favours replacement.

Replacement makes sense in a narrow set of cases: a wheel cracked through a structural section, severe impact damage that compromised the barrel, or a rim so far out of round that straightening can't bring it back to spec safely. We'll tell you plainly if your wheel is in that category — refinishing a wheel that shouldn't be back on the road isn't a service worth selling.

## Preventing the next one

- Give yourself more room than feels necessary when parallel parking — the rim contacts the curb well before the tire looks close
- Approach curbs at a shallow angle rather than straight on
- Take extra care in wet conditions, where a tire can slide the last few centimetres into the concrete
- Consider protective rim tape or a slightly taller tire profile on wheels you care about
